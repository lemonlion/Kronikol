#225 reports that a call made in a sub-step is attributed to its top-level step, so `--step 0.0` finds nothing. It suggests
a marker for each sub-step and a longer list to match them against. The report is right. The suggested fix extends the
mechanism that is at fault, and on the cases measured here it would give some calls a confident wrong answer where
today's answer is vague but true.

## 0. Summary

1. **The issue is right, and it is one symptom.** A call's step is known when the call is logged, is not recorded, and is
   guessed back at report time by counting the step bars the diagram draws. So anything that disturbs a bar disturbs the data:
   a sub-step has none (#225), its text is wrapped (#128), a hook draws one too many (#223), and, unreported, a LightBDD bar shortens an object parameter its row spells out (F17) and
   `ShowStepDelimiters = false` blanks the step of **every call in the run (87 of 87), with no diagnostic**.
2. **The suggested fix would make some answers wrong.** "The deepest open step" reads a stack with one entry per test. With two
   sub-steps running at once, that gives one step's calls to the other, where today's top-level answer is vague but
   true. A marker with no end marker also gives a parent's later calls to the sub-step before them.
3. **The same stack has a fault of its own, which nobody has reported: a failure lands on the wrong step.** Two sub-steps at
   once, the first throws: the first reads passed, the second reads failed with the first's message, and `Failures.md`, the
   file every reader is told to open first, names the second. A sub-step left running also swallows the next Gherkin step and
   its bar. The wiki does advise running sub-steps one after another (`Step-Tracking.md:400`), so these are shapes the product warns about and does not refuse.
4. **An ingest has a fault of this kind that nobody has reported: a documented input loses every call's step.** A tests file's `background: true` row (`Ingesting-External-Captures.md:146`) draws no bar, by design, and the walk over the bars still counts it, so every bar is one out and every call in the scenario reads no step, with nothing in the report to say so. An assertion before the first step and a step with no time do the same (F15). R2 takes those rows out of the count.
5. **The fix:** give a step an identity, let the current step follow the flow of execution, have each record name its step when
   it is logged, and turn that name into an address once the tree is final. A bar then only draws. No public API changes, and the
   weaver is untouched.
6. **Three releases, with an order only where one is measured.** **R2** (patch), a response's step, the walk's matcher and the rows that draw no bar, closes #128, F15 but for a leading assertion row in a capture (`Y2`), and F16, and needs nothing: it passes the core suite on today's main, and it can go first. #223's plan
   (`STEP_IDENTITY_PLAN.md`) adds the identity, the pairing and the stamp, at the top-level step, and keeps no flow. **R1** (patch) needs that: the current step follows the flow and the stamp reads it, fixing the wrong-step failure. While steps are paired by position, a stamp reads the wrong row, and with no stamp that reads the flow, two steps at once put one's calls on the other's row. **R3** (minor), the
   whole path and the readers, closes #225, and needs R1 alone, since #223's R1 now gives each scenario its own Background rows, so a sub-step's address there names a row the report lists (§7.1). #225's symptom stays on an ingest lane, which draws a bar for level 0 alone and flattens the rest (F14, Q6). A sub-step's own bar waits on Q2.
7. **The prototype works, after independent reviews broke it.** My first cuts passed every probe of mine and showed no disagreement on nine real
   suites. A reviewer given only the code and the patch then found 11 problems, three of them regressions: a long-lived consumer's calls read the
   step that started its host, a late `[Step]` became a phantom top-level step, and steps paired by position read the wrong row. That changed the design: a
   record trusts a flow only while its step is open, and the plan's order became a hard dependency. A second review, of #223's third cut, measured two faults in the flow that plan had, and one showed R1 could not ship without the stamp's reading. A third, of the two prototypes built as one before the audit's fixes, found eight more regressions (§8.4), and an audit of the plan against the code found a frame that could close, a service's thread that lost its nesting, and a row R1 itself would make wrong: each is now fixed and has a probe. Two more reviews broke R2's matcher, in two ways and then in seven, and it was redesigned (§8.4). On the final cut, #223's prototype at `638f275d` with this plan's layer and R2, each review's probes read as this plan says, and the issue's own commands answer. On BreakfastProvider's four lanes, 825 scenarios, no call moved to another top-level step and none lost its step, and 838 calls read a sub-step. 22 of 25 diagrams are identical, and the other three are faults fixed. The lanes around step tracking, every example suite and the core suite pass (§8.1). ## 2. How a call gets its step today

1. **While the test runs.** `StepCollector.StartStep` pushes the step on a per-test stack (`StepCollector.cs:70-78`). If the
   stack was empty and `ShowStepDelimiters` is on, it logs a marker: a record in the same FIFO as the calls, holding the
   PlantUML of a bar (`:81-101`, `TrackingDiagramOverride.cs:70`). A call is logged through
   `RequestResponseLogger.Log` (`RequestResponseLogger.cs:60-104`) and carries no step. At that moment the collector knows
   which step is open. Nothing asks it.
2. **When the step ends.** `CompleteStep` pops whatever is on top of the stack and adds it to the step now on top, or to
   the test's list (`StepCollector.cs:138-152`). A sub-step takes its index when it completes, and a tracked assertion
   takes one when it runs (`:204-236`), in the same list.
3. **When the report is drawn.** The adapters build the final tree. Reqnroll and BDDfy pair their own steps with the collected
   ones by position and copy `SubSteps` and `Attachments` (`ScenarioInfoEnumerableExtensions.cs:100-125`,
   `ScenarioInfoExtensions.cs:104-126`), LightBDD merges its own tree with the collected one by position
   (`FeatureResultExtensions.cs:262-268`), and `BackgroundStepsDetector` moves shared prefixes to `b0`, `b1`.
4. **Attribution.** `AttributeInteractionsToSteps` walks each test's records in order. The *n*th step marker opens the *n*th
   top-level step, provided the bar's PlantUML contains the step's text; otherwise that stretch gets `null` and a
   `StepAttributionMismatch` (`ReportGenerator.cs:5381-5441`, `:5463-5471`).
5. **Readers.** The data files write the result per record. `kronikol merge` reads it back and never derives it again
   (`MergeableReportReader.cs:616`). No marker survives into a data file (`ReportGenerator.cs:5341`).

## 4. The underlying problem

**The step a call was made under is known when the call is logged, is not recorded, and is guessed back afterwards from
the diagram's drawing commands.**

A step bar does three jobs at once: it draws (a bar), it marks a position (the *n*th), and it identifies (the text inside
it). Anything that disturbs the drawing therefore disturbs the data:

| What disturbs the bar | What happens to the data | Tracked as |
|---|---|---|
| A sub-step has no bar | Its calls cannot name it | #225 |
| The bar's text is wrapped or escaped for PlantUML | The text no longer matches, and the calls read no step | #128 |
| A `[Step]` in a hook draws one bar too many | Every later bar is one out, and every call reads no step | #223 (its attribution half) |
| Bars are switched off | Every call in the run reads no step | F1, unreported |
| A row the tests file lists draws no bar | Every later bar is one out, and every call reads no step | F15, unreported |
| An adapter draws a parameter differently on the bar and on the row | The calls under that bar read no step | F17, unreported |
| The failure of all this is recorded late | Nobody learns of it | #129 |

Beneath that is a second fault of the same kind. **A step has no identity, and "the current step" has no owner.** The
collector keeps one stack per test, which assumes one flow of control. So with two flows it mis-nests steps (F3), pops the
wrong one (F2), hands a Gherkin step to a sub-step left running (F4), and files assertions under whatever is deepest (F5).
The adapters then pair steps by position for want of anything better, which is #223's other half.

Attribution has been told to be careful about all this: "a null `stepPath` and a diagnostic rather than a confident wrong
answer" (`ReportGenerator.cs:5374`). That is the right standard, and any fix here has to meet it.

## 5. Why not the issue's suggestion

| The suggestion needs | What happens | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A marker for each sub-step | A marker opens a stretch and nothing closes it, so `GET /own/after`, made in the parent after its sub-step ended, reads as the sub-step's. It takes a begin and an end marker, each a pair of records today, in the stream every reader has to skip | s6; `TrackingDiagramOverride.cs:70` |
| The *n*th marker to pair with the *n*th step, depth first | True only when steps do not overlap. With two at once, markers arrive left, right, and the walk would give left's later calls to right: a wrong answer where today's is vague but true. Reasoned from s16 and s14 and not built, though reading the deepest open step does the same (`results/probes-deepest-open.txt`) | INFERRED |
| Text to match, for each marker | #128's fault, on every sub-step | s8, s10 |
| A new `DiagramMarkerKind` | New public surface, exported by name, that `PlantUmlCreator.cs:267`, `MergeableReportReader.cs:649-656` and Kronikol4J must each handle, for something that is not a drawing | READ |
| Bars or markers to exist | Tied to `ShowStepDelimiters` unless split from it. F1 stays, or the option stops meaning what it says | RUN |
| The tree to match the order of arrival | The tree is rebuilt by each adapter, and LightBDD's is its own native list plus collected extras (`FeatureResultExtensions.cs:262-268`) | READ |
| Nothing to change at the front of the stream | One extra top-level marker from a hook still shifts every path after it | #223 |

It also has to stamp nothing, which looks cheaper. It is not: the markers cost more records than a stamp costs an integer,
and every one of the faults in §4 stays.

The opposite shortcut, writing the path string on the record when it is logged, cannot work either. The final address is not
known then: an index is taken at completion, assertions share the numbering, the adapters rebuild the tree, and the
background split turns `0.1` into `b0.1` only at the end.

## 6. The fix

Two changes do the work, and the rest follows from them. **A step gets an identity, and the current step follows the flow
of execution rather than a per-test stack (§6.1, §6.2). A record names its step when it is logged, and the report turns
that name into an address once the tree is final (§6.3, §6.4).** After that a bar only draws.

The public API does not change: `StartStep`, `CompleteStep` and `CompleteStepAsync` keep their signatures, so woven
assemblies keep working and the weaver is untouched (`async` is not part of a method's signature). Every new member is
internal, and `Kronikol.csproj` already opens internals to each adapter.

### 6.1 A step has an identity

`CollectedStep` gets an id, from a process-wide counter in `StartStep`, and a `Parent`. #223's prototype calls it `CollectedStep.Id`, and this plan's layer renames it `Ordinal`, which a release need not: that plan says the names are still open between the two. `ToScenarioStep` copies it to an internal,
never-serialised `ScenarioStep.CollectedId`, and each adapter copies it onto the step it builds, through #223's pairing. The shape was agreed
with that session, which also closes a framework's own step by its id. A per-test counter restarts when `ClearSteps` runs, so a stale stamp could read a new
step, which that session's review found.

A step takes its place in its parent's `SubSteps`, or the test's top-level list, **when it starts**, so siblings read in the order they began and
a step's index no longer depends on when it finished. On a sequential test nothing moves (RUN, §8.3). A step still running when the report is
drawn is listed with no result (`ScenarioStep.Status` is nullable) and no duration. A step that is not done has failed at nothing and passed at
nothing, and today it vanishes with everything under it (F8). #223's plan lists a before-hook's open step as passed (its T24), so the two differ until R1 lands: Q10.

### 6.2 The current step follows the flow

An `AsyncLocal<CollectedStep?>` holds the step this flow is inside, and a step notes when its caller has moved on (`Left`), which for a woven async
step is as soon as its stub returns. A flow is **inside a step** if it holds that step or an open ancestor of it. The rules:

| Moment | What happens |
|---|---|
| `StartStep` | The parent is the flow's nearest open step. With none, it depends on what the flow holds. A **straggler**, whose steps have all ended, goes under whatever is running now, as the stack had it. A flow that **left its steps** (it holds the test's `OutOfItsSteps` marker, as its caller moved on) takes the step last opened whose caller has not moved on, which passes over a step left running (F4), and with none is top-level. A flow that **knows nothing of this test** (a service's thread with the context suppressed) takes the same, and with none goes under whatever is running, as the stack had it: a `[Step]` on a service's thread under two async steps stays nested (`S5`, and the third review's `H11`). The flow moves to the new step |
| A woven step ends (`CompleteStep(bool, ...)`) | Completes the flow's nearest open step, by identity, and moves the flow to its parent. With none, the step last opened whose caller has not moved on. With none of those either, the last step open that is not a frame, even one left running, as the stack would |
| The public API ends a step (`CompleteStep(testId, ...)`) | Last in, first out, as it has always been, among steps whose caller has not moved on, and failing that the last step open that is not a frame. A step left running otherwise closes itself. On top of #223's R1 the adapters use `CompleteFrame`, which closes a framework's own step by id |
| A woven async step | `CompleteStepAsync` becomes a plain method. It moves the caller's flow back to the parent at once, marks the step `Left`, and hands an async helper the step to complete. The step's own body keeps it across its awaits. The next step the caller starts is therefore a sibling. It does nothing when the stub opened nothing, as when no test identity resolved |
| A woven bypass (`SkipIf`) | `BypassStep` closes the step and the flow stays on it, because the weave sends every bypass through its stub's completion: the bypass's own `ret` is in the list it rewrites (`StepWeaver.cs:319-324`, `:527-532`). That completion finds a bypassed step in the flow, moves the flow to its parent and closes nothing. Today it closes the next step up, or a sibling left running (F13, the third review's `F16`, `F17`, `F18`, `G14`). #224's patch as kept here did the same on its own chain, and #226's weave and #224's latest patch stop the pass-through: §7.1 says how they fit |
| A hand-called `BypassStep(testId, ...)` | No stub follows, so the flow moves up at once, as a completion would |
| An assertion or an attachment | The flow's nearest open step, otherwise the step last opened, as the stack had it |
| Closing a step twice | The second does nothing |
| On top of #223's R1: a framework's own step, which that plan calls a frame | Its parent is the frame open now, or none, and never a step method. That is #223's rule, and the flow cannot replace it: Reqnroll carries what a sync binding leaves in the context on to the next, so a step opened by hand in a sync hook would hold every row under it (RUN, `rq` s6). Only `CompleteFrame` closes a frame: a sync completion that would land on one takes the step last opened that is not a frame, a `CompleteStepAsync` that finds the flow in a frame leaves it alone (`FlowIsInAFrame`), and a bypass that finds a frame closes nothing, as #223's prototype has it (`0ab1efa2`). That plan's list of steps left running has no job here, since such a step is simply still open |

Why `CompleteStepAsync` has to be a plain method: the woven stub runs `StartStep` in its caller's frame, so the caller's flow
holds the child until something moves it back, and an `async` method's changes to an `AsyncLocal` are undone when it returns.
#226's draft plan (`STEP_METHOD_SHAPES_PLAN.md`) adds no overload: it hands a `ValueTask` step to this same method through `.AsTask()`, in the
stub's frame, so this rule reaches it with nothing further.

This is a well-trodden shape. `System.Diagnostics.Activity.Current` is an `AsyncLocal` with a parent link, restored when an
activity stops. Allure's .NET library holds its step context in an `AsyncLocal`, and its design notes record the same trap for
the older model: "May lead to an invalid steps structure if Step 1 is continued in a context modified by Step 2"
([allure-csharp#371](https://github.com/allure-framework/allure-csharp/pull/371)) (DOC). Reqnroll carries the context from
one binding to the next on purpose, "the next step continues with the ExecutionContext that the previous step finished with"
([`BindingDelegateInvoker.cs`](https://github.com/reqnroll/Reqnroll/blob/main/Reqnroll/Bindings/BindingDelegateInvoker.cs))
(DOC), but nothing here depends on that holding. A write made inside an async hook or frame is undone on return, and such a step is
then found as the last opened (RUN, §8.4).

### 6.3 A record names its step when it is logged

`RequestResponseLogger.Log` is the funnel: `LogPair` goes through it (`RequestResponseLogger.cs:181`, `:190`), and a held call
has been through it once (`:107`). After redaction and the cap, and **before** the `DocumentFlow` hold, so a held call keeps the
step it was made under, it stamps two internal fields, #223's `RequestResponseLog.OpenStep` and a new `OpenStepIsItsFlows`, which says whether the record's own flow answered. A step's bar is stamped too, on #223's cut, and only to name the bar's step. The answer, in order:

1. **This flow is inside an open step of this test:** its nearest open step. Never a step that has ended: a loop started in a `Given` handles later
   messages long after, and those calls are the later steps' doing (Q1, §8.4's finding 2).
2. **Otherwise, from what is open.** One chain open: the deepest. Several branches open: their nearest shared ancestor, which
   is the most that can honestly be said. A call a service makes on its own thread is this case: `TestServer` does not carry
   the context across by default ([`PreserveExecutionContext` defaults to false](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/microsoft.aspnetcore.testhost.testserveroptions.preserveexecutioncontext)) (DOC). Several running with nothing shared, as two top-level steps at once: **no step**, stamped as an id no step has, since naming the last one started would be a guess (the third review's `F03`).
3. **Nothing open:** the last top-level step, the bar drawn above the call. This keeps today's answer for a call a test makes between two steps,
   and it does not depend on bars. "No step" is the tempting alternative and #147 asks for it after the last step, but #223's review measured it taking
   correct calls from every lane (BDDfy's async calls, whose frame closes at the first `await`, and a plain lane's call between two steps), and
   Kronikol4J's walk gives today's answer too. #147 stays open.

#223's stamp is set only while a step is open, and reads the top of its stack, and an unstamped record reads the step before it. On that stack the top level gives the same answers, since two steps at once nest there. R1 makes them siblings, and a stamp reading the step last opened then puts the first one's call on the second's row (RUN, §7.1), so this reading goes in with R1. Rule 3
adds one case: with bars off and no record since, there is no step before it to read, and `LastTopLevel` still has it.

**The trace join.** Rule 2 reads what is open, and that is the wrong question for a service's onward call while the caller's own request is waiting. A parent calls a service while a child runs: the service's onward call reads the child, while the parent's own record reads the parent. So at report time, a request stamped from what was open, and not by its own flow, takes the path of the innermost request still waiting on the same Kronikol trace from another party (`ThroughTheirCalls`). The trace is the one the tracking handler already sends (`TestTrackingMessageHandler.cs:238-239`), and `CallerName` tells the two parties apart. A request with no such parent keeps rule 2's answer, and a call whose parent has been answered has no parent, so a late call does not move and Q1 stands. It reads records the report already has, and needs only the new `OpenStepIsItsFlows`. `NestingRule` has the same trace clause, and its caller clause guesses under concurrency, so the join does not use that one.

| A service's onward call (`probes-service/`, `results/service-probes.txt`) | Published | Rule 2 alone | With the join |
|---|---|---|---|
| `S1`: two sub-steps at once, each calling the service | Both `0` | `onward-of-right=0`, the parent they share, and `onward-of-left-slow=0.0` | Each its own: `onward-of-right=0.1`, `onward-of-left-slow=0.0` |
| `S2`: a parent's own call while a child runs | `onward-of-parent-own=0` | `0.0`, the child: one call split across two steps | `0` |
| `S3`: two top-level steps at once | Both `0`, as the steps nest | `onward-of-fast=1`, the wrong row: rule 2 fell to the last top-level | Each its own: `onward-of-fast=0`, `onward-of-top-slow=1` |

With the third review's `F03` fixed, a record in `S3`'s shape with no waiting request reads no step, where it read the last top-level. The join belongs in R1: `S3` is a regression R1 would otherwise create, as the row it reads is one R1 makes.

The stamp is one dictionary read per record and, in a test that has steps, a short lock and one `AsyncLocal` read. Measured on a loaded
machine on the cut at `10bbbe81` (`perf/`, `results/perf-final.txt`, best of 5, two rounds, against published): a record costs 1.4 to 1.7 µs today, and the noise is the size of the effect. No step in the test: -0.01 and +0.12 µs. A step open in this flow: -0.13 and +0.17 µs. A step open and this flow outside it: +0.07 and +0.05 µs, with two steps open, and the prototype's LINQ in that path grows with what is open (§10). So nothing measurable at this size. A real call takes thousands of times that. `kronikol ingest` never opens a collector step, so its records
stay unstamped.

## 10. Risks and traps

- **Rows move when top-level steps overlap (Q9).** Two `[Step]`s started together at the top level were one row and its sub-step, and become two rows, so every later step's address is one higher (`P5`: `/orders` from `1` to `2`), and the second draws a bar it did not have (§6.6). This is F3's fix at the top level. It is also the tree #223's reviewer called a regression on that plan's cut, where `/fast` read the other row. Here each call reads its own, but anything pinned to those addresses sees them move, so R1's changelog says so.
- **A hook's `[Step]` that awaits a Gherkin step lists it as a sibling** (`R05`). A frame's parent is the frame open now (§6.2), so `Given` reads beside `Seed` and not under it. Its calls are right. The flow does not settle this: with a frame's parent read from the flow the row stayed where it is (RUN, output not kept), since Reqnroll runs a binding in the context it holds from the binding before, and the frame's hook never sees that flow.
- **A service's call with no trace to join.** The limit this plan first stated, a parent's own service-side call split across two steps, is gone with the trace join (§6.3). What remains is a call that carries no Kronikol trace, or whose caller's request has already been answered. It reads rule 2's answer: the deepest step of one chain, the step shared by several, or no step when several run with nothing shared. Each is vague at worst.
- **A runtime-async `[Step]` has no stub** (`runtime-async=on` and `DOTNET_RuntimeAsync=1`, here on SDK 10.0.300). #226's plan recommends bracketing such a step, its Q8, where it first recommended refusing one, and states the limit: with no stub it never calls `CompleteStepAsync`, and it cannot move the phase for its caller. So `Left` is never set. Inside a step that changes nothing, failures included. At the top level, `Task.WhenAll(Fast(), Slow())` keeps published's tree, `0 Fast`, `0.0 Slow`, `1 next`, and each call reads its own (`fast=0 slow=0.0 next=1`), so it is a tree that says less and no wrong row (RUN on the final cut with that patch, `results/runtime-async-with-226-305e03ff.txt`). A fact pins this once that plan lands.
- **What R2 still leaves to the walk.** A bar whose keyword is more than one word needs the row to hold it. On a bar with no keyword, a text with its first word gone reads as that bar, which published does too on a plain label. A bar cannot be told from a row's own where they differ past a cut (`E1`), by the same text under another keyword or none, a row with no keyword under a keyword's bar, two rows with no keyword apart only in their first letter, a wrapped first line that goes on with a blank display line and a pipe, or two texts `Build` draws alike (§6.4). Each is a read published refuses on that label. No walk runs where a row carries an id, so they stay only where a walk stays: ingest, the Cucumber lane, hand-built rows, rows an adapter pairs by position, and Kronikol4J's format.
- **A foreign record's cost.** On a thread that is not a step's, `OrdinalOfOpenStep` is O(open²) under the test's lock in the prototype. The third review measured 800 calls with 800 children open at 1,775 ms against 75 ms. A release keeps a count of open children on each step, and that cost goes. The same review measured `steps` on 4,000 rows at 2.4 to 2.7 s against 1.5 to 2.1 s: a release counts a subtree in one pass. `review-probes-third/perf/` holds that probe's source, and its output was not kept.
- **A step tree 28 levels deep stops the report** with a `JsonException` when `TestRunReport.krkl` is written, on published and on the prototype alike; 25 levels is fine (the third review's `perf`, output not kept). R1 changes nothing here, since nesting is not deeper than it was, and it should be filed on its own.
- **A context that outlives its test.** A host started inside a test inherits that flow. Rule 1 checks that the flow's step belongs to the record's test and is
  open, so a call the host makes for another test, or after its step ended, reads what is open. One that arrives after the test ended is moved to
  the run's background by `BackgroundAttribution.Expire`, whose id has no steps, so the stamp is simply unused (READ).
- **`GetSteps` while a flow is alive.** It walks the tree under the state's lock, and every writer takes it. Places taken at start mean a reader can see an
  open step, which §6.1 gives no result.
- **`IsOpen` read without a lock** in `LeaveFlowStep`: the read is of the flow's own step, in its own flow, before anything can complete it. Harmless, but a release
  should say so in a comment or take the lock.
- **Two addressings.** `CollectedStepPaths` and `ReportIndex.AllSteps` must agree, which is why R3 has a round-trip fact.
- **A `Task`-returning `[Step]` that throws before it returns a task.** The async weave has no `try`: it rewrites each `ret`
  (`StepWeaver.cs:521-533`), so such a method never reaches `CompleteStepAsync`, and its step stays open and the flow stays on it (RUN by #226's session, its
  shape C05, and the third review's `F15`). Today the scenario's whole tree is lost, and on the prototype the tree stays, with the next step nested under the abandoned one. #226's draft plan
  gives the async weave a `try`, and #224's R1 (its F20) a catch on a Task stub that is not `async`. Either removes this.
- **A bypass's completion** (§6.2, §7.1). R1 cures F13 for a `[Step]` parent as well as a frame, which an earlier draft of this plan left to #226. The cure relies on the weave's pass-through, so it is the one piece of R1 tied to the weaver's behaviour.
- **A held call.** `DetachedFlow` re-stores a record it held (`DetachedFlow.cs:50`, `:117`), through `with`, so the stamp survives. It is why the stamp goes before the hold.
- **The prototype is not the release.** It has no tests of its own, and it has LINQ in the shared-ancestor path and takes the lock on every record, which a release can avoid while a flow is inside its own step. `prototype/on-223-638f275d.patch` is the whole of this plan on #223's cut, and it is one change: it shows the design and the fit, and each release is cut from it with its facts. `prototype/r2-alone.patch` is R2 as it would land.
